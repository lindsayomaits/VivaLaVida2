General Event Details

What is the Viva la Vida Festival?

Hosted by the Mexic-Arte Museum, it is Austin's largest and longest-running Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead) event. This year marks the 43rd annual Viva la Vida Festival & Parade!

When is the event?

Saturday, October 24, 2026. The grand procession (parade) kicks off at 12:00 PM and runs until 1:00 PM. The main street festival runs from 12:00 PM to 6:00 PM. If you want to walk with Reilly, meet us at 6th Street & Red River Street at 10:00 AM. See the full Day-of Schedule above!

Where is the festival located?

Downtown Austin! The parade lines up near 6th & Red River and travels about ¾ mile west down Historic 6th Street to the main festival grounds at 4th Street and Congress Avenue (near Republic Square Park).

How much does it cost?

Nada! Admission to the festival and participation in the parade is completely free and open to all ages.

Can we take public transportation? (Please do!)

We strongly encourage it! Downtown parking fills up fast and 6th Street will be closed for the parade, so taking the train or bus is the easiest, least stressful way to get there.

  • MetroRail: The Downtown Station at 4th St & Trinity St is a short walk from our 6th & Red River meet-up.
  • CapMetro buses: Many routes stop downtown near Congress Avenue. Park & Ride lots like Tech Ridge or South Congress let you park outside downtown and ride in.
  • Rideshare or drop-off: Ask to be dropped on 5th or 7th Street, since 6th Street will be blocked off.
  • Plan your trip: Find routes and times on the CapMetro website or the CapMetro app.

Where should we park?

There is no assigned parking for parade participants, and downtown parking fills quickly. Give yourself the 9:00 hour to park or get dropped off. Here are some options:

  • City Garages: Austin Convention Center, City Hall Garage, One Texas Center, Palmer Events Center, and RiverSouth.
  • Discounted Parking: Mexic-Arte Museum offers special discounted parking ($10 for 4 hours) at the Frost Bank Tower Garage.
  • Drop-off Tip: 6th Street will be blocked off! Parade organizers recommend dropping people off on 5th or 7th Street and walking to the staging area.
  • Public Transit: Capital Metro buses and rideshares are highly recommended. CapMetro buses drop off near 4th Street and Congress Avenue, and transit centers like South Congress or Tech Ridge Park & Ride offer easy bus access.

What happens if it rains?

We'll communicate if it's cancelled, but you can also keep up on the Mexic-Arte Museum event page.

What is the exact meeting location and time for Reilly participants?

Meet us at the corner of 6th Street & Red River Street at 10:00 AM. Look for the Reilly crew! We'll take a group photo at 11:00 AM, so don't be late!

Absolutely! At the 10:00 AM meet-up we'll hand out decorations for bikes, wagons, strollers and more, so come a little early to deck them out.

No. All minors must be with an adult. Parents and guardians are entirely responsible for their children and must stay with their kids the whole time. This is a family-participation event, not a drop-off childcare service.

We encourage everybody to wear a Reilly Elementary shirt! You can also come in Día de los Muertos face paint to match the spirit of the event. Our theme is Keep Austin Weird, so you have full creative license!

  • Smiles! Bring your best Reilly spirit.
  • A stroller or wagon, if you wish.
  • Food, snacks & water you may need.
  • Ear protection, if you wish. The parade gets quite loud!
  • Your costume and/or costume makeup.
  • Comfy shoes for the ¾-mile route.
  • Weather protection: sunscreen and hats for sun, or rain gear if showers are in the forecast.

Should families bring their own water and snacks?

We'll share food with Reilly families right after our 11:00 AM group photo, but please bring water and any food you may want. The festival has food vendors as well.

¿Sabías Que?

Did you know? Cool cultural nuggets to share with your kids on the way downtown!

This year's theme: La Ofrenda

An ofrenda is an altar families build to welcome loved ones' spirits home, with photos, candles and their favorite foods. In this year's parade, artist-made altar pieces are carried down 6th Street and assembled into one giant altar at the festival!

Flowers that light the way

Orange marigolds, called cempasúchil, are the signature flower of the holiday. Their bright color and strong scent are said to guide spirits back to their families.

Happy skulls, not spooky ones

Smiling calaveras (decorated skulls) celebrate life instead of fearing death. The elegant skeleton lady, La Catrina, was first drawn over 100 years ago by Mexican artist José Guadalupe Posada.

Papel picado

These colorful cut-paper banners flutter in the breeze, and their delicate paper is a reminder of how precious and fragile life is.

Two days of remembering

Día de los Muertos is celebrated November 1 and 2. It blends ancient Indigenous traditions from Mexico with Catholic holidays. Families often share pan de muerto, a sweet bread decorated with "bones" made of dough.

Don't miss at the festival

Bouncing low-rider cars, live music and dance, hands-on art activities, and the Muertos Mercado with 20+ vendors selling Day of the Dead crafts and gifts.
